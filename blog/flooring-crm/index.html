Flooring CRM: What It Is, What It Needs and How to Use It

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 18, 2026

A flooring CRM is the one place where every lead, quote and sold job is recorded, with where the customer came from and what happens next. It matters for two reasons. It stops leads and quotes from being forgotten during a sale that takes weeks. And it is the only way to know what your marketing spend is producing, because it ties each sold job back to the ad, search or referral that started it.

We run flooring showrooms ourselves and built a CRM for our own stores, so this is written from daily use. We are not selling one here.

Why a flooring business needs a CRM

A flooring sale is not one moment. Someone calls or walks in, a measure gets scheduled, a quote goes out, the customer thinks about it, and weeks later the job is sold and installed. Leads arrive by phone, web form, text and in person, and more than one salesperson is involved.

Without one shared record, three things happen:

  • Quotes go out and nobody follows up.
  • Two people call the same customer, or nobody does.
  • At the end of the month you know what you spent on marketing but not what it sold.

What a flooring CRM should include

  • Every way a lead arrives. Phone calls, website forms, texts and walk-ins all land in the same list.
  • The source of each lead. Google search, Google Ads, Facebook, a referral, a past customer, a yard sign. This one field is what makes the rest of the reporting possible.
  • Stages that match how flooring sells. New, contacted, measure scheduled, quote sent, sold, installed.
  • The quote. Product, rooms and the amount.
  • Who owns it. One salesperson per lead.
  • A next step with a date. Every open lead has one, and overdue ones are easy to see.
  • The sold amount. So reports show dollars, not only counts.
  • A duplicate warning. The same customer should not be entered twice.
  • A daily list. What needs attention today, without digging.
  • Reports by source, by salesperson and by store. Leads, quotes and sold jobs for each.
  • Your data, exportable. You can take your customer list with you if you leave.

What we learned building our own

  • The source has to be trustworthy. When a lead comes in from an ad, the ad platform's record sets the source, not a guess made at the counter. Salespeople can fill in the source for walk-ins, and a manager can correct it.
  • The daily screen should be short. Ours shows two things: money about to slip, meaning quotes going cold, and salespeople falling behind on follow-up. Nothing else.
  • A morning email does more than a dashboard. People open email. Each morning ours lists what needs action that day.
  • Count calls, not only forms. Most flooring leads still start with a phone call. We track which calls on our marketing numbers turned into a lead.

How to use a flooring CRM day to day

  1. Enter every lead the day it arrives. A lead that is not in the list does not exist next week.
  2. Ask how they heard about you, and record it. Every time.
  3. Give every open lead a next step and a date. A quote with no follow-up date is a quote you have stopped selling.
  4. Start each morning with the list of what is due. Five minutes.
  5. Mark jobs sold, with the amount. This is the step most stores skip, and it is the one that makes the marketing numbers real.
  6. Review sold jobs by source once a month.

How a CRM tells you what your marketing spend is doing

Ad platforms report clicks, calls and "conversions." None of those is a sold job. The CRM closes the gap, because it follows each lead to the end.

  1. Each lead is recorded with its source.
  2. Each sold job is marked with its amount.
  3. Once a month, put what you spent on each source next to the jobs that source sold.
  4. Divide. That is your cost per sold job for each source.

Here is the math with made-up numbers. A store spends $1,200 on Google Ads in a month. The CRM shows 20 leads from those ads, and five became sold jobs. That is $240 per sold job. The same store spends $600 on another channel that produced 15 leads and one sold job, which is $600 per sold job. The second channel looked cheaper per lead and cost more than twice as much per sale. You can only see that with the sold jobs recorded.

One honest warning from our own numbers. When we first tied ad spend to sold jobs across our own businesses, we could connect less than a fifth of the spend to a sold job. The rest was not wasted. It simply could not be judged, because the source was missing on the lead or the sale was never marked. Treat a channel with spend and no recorded sales as "cannot tell yet," not as a failure, and fix the recording first. Start with a spreadsheet if you need to

The habit matters more than the tool. A shared spreadsheet with these columns covers a one-showroom store:

ColumnWhat goes in it
DateThe day the lead arrived
Name and phoneThe customer
SourceHow they heard about you
ProjectProduct and rooms
SalespersonWho owns it
QuoteThe amount quoted
Next step and dateWhat happens next, and when
StatusOpen, sold or lost
Sold amountFilled in when it sells

Move to a CRM when leads start slipping, or when more than two people work from the list.

Flooring CRM or flooring software?

They do different jobs. Flooring business software usually handles estimating, orders, inventory and job costing once a sale is made. A CRM handles the customer side before the sale: leads, follow-up and quotes. Many stores use both. Ask whether the two can share customer and job information, so nobody types it twice.

Questions to ask before you choose one

  1. Can I export all my customers and history at any time?
  2. Do calls, texts and website forms come in on their own, or does someone type them?
  3. Can it record the source of every lead, and can I report sold jobs by source?
  4. What does it cost per person, and what is the notice to cancel?
  5. Will my salespeople actually use it on a busy Saturday?

FAQ

What is a flooring CRM?

A flooring CRM is the one place where a flooring store records every lead, quote and sold job, along with where the customer came from and what happens next. CRM stands for customer relationship management. For a showroom it is a shared, up-to-date list of who asked about a floor and where each one stands.

Does a small flooring store need a CRM?

It needs the habit more than the software. A one-showroom store can start with a shared spreadsheet that records each lead, its source, the quote, the next step and whether it sold. Move to a CRM when leads are slipping through, or when more than two people need to work from the same list.

What should a flooring CRM track?

Every lead with its source, the product and room, the salesperson, the measure date, the quote amount, the next follow-up date, whether it sold and for how much. With those few fields you can see sold jobs by source, by salesperson and by month.

What is the difference between a flooring CRM and flooring software?

Flooring business software usually handles estimating, orders, inventory and job costing after the sale is made. A CRM handles the customer side before the sale: leads, follow-up and quotes. Many stores use both, and it helps when the two share customer and job information.

How does a CRM show which marketing is working?

It connects each sold job back to the source of the lead. Put your monthly spend for each source beside the jobs that source sold, and you get cost per sold job. That number tells you what to grow and what to cut. Clicks and platform reports cannot.
