Marketing for flooring stores: what actually brings in showroom visits

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026

The marketing that brings flooring customers into a showroom is mostly local and mostly unglamorous: a complete Google Business Profile, a website that answers real buying questions for your towns, a phone number for every channel so you know what rang, and ads judged by the jobs they sell instead of the clicks they get. We run three Carpet One showrooms (Kirksville and Springfield, Missouri, and Columbia Rug in Peoria, Illinois), and that is the order we work in.

Start with the goal: a showroom visit, then a sold job

Flooring customers are already in the market. Nobody buys carpet for three bedrooms on impulse. They are deciding who to trust and whether the price will be fair. Plenty of them put off visiting a showroom because they are nervous about the salesperson.

So the job of your marketing is not to inspire anyone. It is to make one local store feel like the safe, obvious place to go look. On our store sites, the main asks are a phone call or "text me the details," then a visit. The estimate form is still there, but it is not the main event.

And the scoreboard is sold jobs. Every lead gets a source when it comes in, every sold job keeps it, and a channel is judged by what it cost divided by the jobs it sold. A tap on "get directions" is a good sign. It is not a lead, and we do not count it as one.

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a shopper sees

In a "flooring store near me" search, the map results usually show up before any website. In October 2026 we recorded cleanup work across nine Business Profiles in our businesses. The work was simple and almost all free:

  • A full description that sounds like the owner talking.
  • Every service you sell and install, listed in the words customers use. Our Peoria store went from 9 listed services to 22 without adding anything new to the store.
  • Links on https.
  • Holiday hours set ahead of time.
  • A post every week.
  • A reply to every review.

Our full setup is in Google Business Profile for flooring stores: the setup we use.

A website that answers questions, with your towns in it

A flooring website has two jobs: answer the questions people type into Google, and make the visit feel easy.

On October 26, 2026, we moved our three Carpet One stores off the corporate co-op websites and onto one website per store that we control. Ads, organic search and the Business Profiles now point to the same site, so a fix to a page is a fix everywhere.

On October 27, 2026, we added a Flooring Guides section to each store site with practical buying questions and local service information. Some guides included FAQ markup. That records work performed, not an established search improvement. One example is our Springfield store's vinyl plank installation guide.

Google requires no special AI markup or answer length. FAQ rich results ended May 7, 2026. Keep questions and answers when they help customers.

What to put on your site, at minimum:

  • Your address, hours and main phone number, the same as on your Business Profile and directory listings.
  • Real photos of your showroom and your work. No stock photos of people who don't work there.
  • A plain explanation of pricing, including which materials, installation, removal and other work your estimate includes.
  • Guide pages that answer the questions your salespeople hear every week.

The full list is in Local SEO for flooring stores: a checklist from people who run three showrooms.

Give every channel its own phone number

If every call rings in on the same number, you cannot tell which channel earned it. At our stores, the ads and each website have their own tracked numbers, and the Business Profile and listings carry the main store line. Every call is logged with the number it came in on, then graded, so a short wrong-number call does not count as a lead. Leads get tied to sold jobs. We get a daily email with the calls, so nobody has to go looking.

Without this, "where did you hear about us" is a guess at the counter.

Judge paid ads by sold jobs, not conversions

Ad platforms count almost anything as a conversion if you let them. We found one of our own store ad accounts counting taps on "get directions" as conversions. The report looked busy and told us nothing about floors sold.

What we do instead:

  • Point ads at the same site that ranks in search, so the whole path is visible.
  • Use the tracked number for that channel in the ads.
  • Look at cost per sold job, not cost per click or cost per conversion.
  • When a channel has spend but no sold job tied to it yet, call it "can't measure." Not working, not failing. Unknown, until the data is there.

A channel can look cheap per lead and still be expensive per sold job, because it rings the phone with people who were never going to buy.

Send Facebook leads straight to follow-up

Facebook lead ad leads often have not seen your showroom yet, so they need a quick, human follow-up. Our October 2026 store workflow described routing these inquiries into our internal CRM alongside calls and website leads. That is an example from our own businesses, not a client software offer. For marketing clients, any later CRM connection depends on the existing system and a separately agreed scope.

Check what AI search says about you

For a suggested check, keep consistent homeowner questions and record the service, model or mode, date, location, complete answers and cited URLs. Compare repeated observations; a mention is not a lead or proof that a change worked. This is a proposed method, not a report of completed recurring checks. FAQ

What is the best marketing for a flooring store?

The best marketing for a flooring store is the kind you can trace to sold jobs. For most local stores that starts with a complete Google Business Profile, a website that answers buying questions for your towns, and a separate phone number for each channel. Ads come next, judged by cost per sold job rather than clicks.

How do I get more people into my flooring showroom?

Make the visit the main goal and make it feel easy. Show real photos of the showroom, your hours and what happens when someone walks in, and explain how pricing works in plain words. Ask for a call or a text first, then the visit. Track calls by channel so you know what is actually bringing people in.

Do Facebook ads work for flooring stores?

Facebook lead ads can bring in leads, but the leads need fast, personal follow-up because many have not seen your store yet. Route them into the same system as your calls and website leads, give them a source, and judge the campaign by how many sold jobs it produced, not by how many forms it collected.

How long does flooring store marketing take to work?

Ads and follow-up can produce calls within weeks. Search work usually takes months to show up in rankings, and longer to show up in sold jobs, because flooring jobs take time to measure, quote and sell. Judge any change on at least a full quarter of data before deciding it worked.
