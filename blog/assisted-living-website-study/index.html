We checked 255 assisted living websites on a phone

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 23, 2026

In November 2026 we opened the home pages of 255 assisted living websites the way a family would, on a phone, and checked for the things a family looks for first. About two in three had a phone number that could be tapped to call. About one in thirteen showed a price. In a speed test of 142 of them, the median score was 53 out of 100 and the median page took 7.2 seconds to show its main content.

We own and run an assisted living and memory care home ourselves, so we know what a family asks on the first call. This is a count of what the websites show, not a report of client results.

The person on the other end is often an adult daughter or son, on a phone, soon after a hospital stay. About four in five of these pages kept the test phone waiting more than four seconds, which Google rates as poor.

What we checked and how

  • The home page check. We fetched each home page once on November 23, 2026, identifying as a phone, and looked in the page for fixed patterns: a tap-to-call link, a dollar figure, a link to a pricing, rates, cost or fees page, tour or visit wording, a form, and the details search engines read.
  • The speed test. We ran Lighthouse, the open tool behind Google's PageSpeed Insights, on its phone setting, once per home page, on a sample of 142 of the sites. The score runs from 0 to 100, and Google calls 90 to 100 good, 50 to 89 needs improvement and 0 to 49 poor.
  • The load time is Largest Contentful Paint: how long until the biggest thing on the screen has appeared. Google's target is 2.5 seconds or less, and it rates more than 4 seconds as poor.

Which homes are in the sample

The websites come from public state licensing records for assisted living homes. Only some states publish a website address in those records, so the sample leans heavily on them: 51% of the 255 sites are in Maryland and 70% are in Maryland or New York. The rest are spread over 30 other states. We left out homes flagged as part of a national chain or a large system. Some of what remains belongs to regional operators we could not identify.

We started with 443 website addresses. We left out the ones we could not load from our test machine, pages with almost no text, and addresses that carried a known chain name. This is not a random sample of American assisted living homes, so read the percentages as a picture of these 255 sites, not a census. We are not publishing the names of any home.

What this check does not tell you

  • Home pages only. A home may publish prices or a tour form on another page. We counted a link from the home page to a pricing page, but we did not read the rest of each site.
  • Patterns, not people. A pattern can miss a price written as words, or a phone link added by a script after the page loads. Expect the true figures to be a little higher than ours for those items.
  • A lab speed test, not real visitors. Google explains that lab data is one simulated load on one device. A family on a newer phone with good signal will often see the page sooner. Our test ran on a cloud server, one run per site, and scores move a few points between runs.
  • It does not measure inquiries. Nothing here shows how many families call, tour or move in.

What to check on your own site

  1. Tap your phone number. Open your site on your own phone. If tapping the number does not start a call, fix that first.
  2. Find the price. Count the taps from the home page to a dollar figure. If there is none, add a starting price and say what changes it.
  3. Ask for a tour. Use your own form or button, after hours, and see who answers and when.
  4. Run the speed test. Use pagespeed.web.dev three times on its phone setting. Large photos are the usual first fix.

Common questions

What should an assisted living website show on its home page?

A family on a phone should see what care you provide, where you are, what it costs, how to tour and a phone number they can tap. In our November 2026 check of 255 assisted living home pages, 69.4% had a phone number that could be tapped and 7.5% showed a dollar figure.

Do assisted living websites publish prices?

Most do not, at least not on the home page. In our check of 255 assisted living home pages, 19 showed a dollar figure and 53 either showed one or linked to a page about pricing, rates, cost or fees. Some homes publish prices deeper in the site, which this check did not look at.

How fast is the typical assisted living website on a phone?

In our lab test of 142 of those home pages, the median phone speed score was 53 out of 100 and the median page took 7.2 seconds to show its main content. Google's target is 2.5 seconds or less.

How do I test my own assisted living website?

Go to pagespeed.web.dev, type in your home page address and read the phone result. Look at the performance score and at Largest Contentful Paint. Run it three times, because the score moves a few points between runs. Then open your site on your own phone and try to call, find the price and ask for a tour.

Does a slow website mean a home is losing families?

Not by itself. This check shows how long a visitor on an ordinary phone waits and whether the basics are on the home page. It does not measure inquiries, tours or move-ins. It tells you where to look first.
