We tested 831 flooring store websites on a phone

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 18, 2026

In November 2026 we ran Google's phone speed test on the home pages of 831 independent flooring store websites in 49 states and Washington, D.C. The median score was 66 out of 100. One site in ten (83 of 831) scored 90 or better, the range Google calls good. The median site took 6.6 seconds to show its main content, and three in four took longer than 4 seconds, which Google rates as poor.

Put plainly: on the phone Google uses for this test, nine out of ten independent flooring store websites are slower than Google's own target, and one in three keeps the shopper waiting more than ten seconds.

What we tested and how

We used Google's public speed test, PageSpeed Insights, on its phone setting. Each store's home page was tested once between November 17 and November 18, 2026.

  • The score is the performance score from 0 to 100. Google colors it good at 90 to 100, needs improvement at 50 to 89 and poor at 0 to 49.
  • The load time is Largest Contentful Paint: how long until the biggest thing on the screen, usually the main photo or headline, has appeared. Google's target is 2.5 seconds or less, and it rates more than 4 seconds as poor.
  • The phone is simulated. Google describes it as a mid-tier device on a mobile network.

Which stores are in the sample

The 831 sites belong to independent flooring retailers on our own list of stores that publish a website and an email address. We tested them in the order they came up on that list. National chains are not included. This is a large sample, but it is not a random one, so read the percentages as a close picture of independent stores, not a census.

We tried 846 sites. Google's test could not read 15 of them, so they are left out. We are not publishing store names. The point is the pattern, not any one store.

What this test does not tell you

  • It is a lab test, not your real visitors. Google explains that lab data is a simulated load on one device and one connection, while field data comes from real visits. A shopper on a new phone with good signal will often see your page sooner than the test did.
  • One run per site. A score can move a few points from run to run. Across 831 sites that evens out. For a single store it matters, so test your own site more than once.
  • Home pages only. Product and gallery pages are often heavier.
  • It does not measure sales. A slow score does not prove a store is losing customers. It shows how long a shopper on an ordinary phone waits before the page is usable.

Why flooring sites tend to be slow

This study recorded the score and the load time, not the causes. In the full reports we have read closely, the same few things came up:

  • Large photos sent to small screens. Flooring is a visual product, so home pages carry many big images, and they are often sent to a phone at desktop size.
  • A rotating slider at the top of the home page. Every slide loads before the shopper sees anything.
  • Page builders and add-ons. Each one adds code the phone has to download and run.
  • Third-party tools that load right away: chat boxes, room visualizers, review widgets and tracking tags.

Test your own site in two minutes

  1. Open pagespeed.web.dev on any computer.
  2. Type in your home page address and run it.
  3. Stay on the phone result. Write down the performance score and Largest Contentful Paint.
  4. Run it two more times and use the middle result.
  5. Do the same for your busiest product page.

If you score 90 or better and your main content shows in 2.5 seconds or less, you are ahead of nine in ten of the independent stores we tested.

What to fix first

  1. The top photo. Size it for a phone screen, save it in a modern format such as WebP, and let the browser choose the right size.
  2. The slider. Replace it with one strong photo and one clear next step.
  3. Tools that load before they are needed. Have the chat box, visualizer and review widget load after the page is usable, or when the shopper taps them.
  4. Add-ons nobody uses. Remove them. Each one costs load time on every visit.
  5. Test again. Change one thing, run the test three times, and keep what moved the number.

Does speed matter for Google?

Google says it highly recommends good Core Web Vitals for success with Search, and that page experience lines up with what its core ranking systems seek to reward. It does not promise that a fast page ranks first. We treat speed as a basic: it will not win a search by itself, but a ten second wait can lose the shopper you already earned.

FAQ

What is a good phone speed score for a flooring store website?

Google's speed test scores a page from 0 to 100 and calls 90 to 100 good, 50 to 89 needs improvement and 0 to 49 poor. For load time, Google's target is 2.5 seconds or less for the main content to appear. In our test of 831 independent flooring store websites, 83 scored 90 or better.

How fast is the typical flooring store website on a phone?

In our November 2026 test, the median independent flooring store home page scored 66 out of 100 and took 6.6 seconds to show its main content in Google's simulated phone test. Half the sites were slower than that.

Does website speed affect where a flooring store shows up on Google?

Google says it highly recommends good Core Web Vitals for success with Search and that page experience lines up with what its core ranking systems seek to reward. It does not promise that a fast page ranks first. Speed is one factor among many, and it also decides how many shoppers wait long enough to see your showroom.

How do I test my own flooring store website?

Go to pagespeed.web.dev, type in your home page address and read the phone result. Look at two numbers: the performance score and Largest Contentful Paint. Run it three times, because the score moves a few points between runs.

Why did my score change when I ran the test again?

The test loads your page fresh each time on a simulated phone and connection, and small differences in server response and third-party scripts move the result. Judge your site by the range you see over several runs, not by one number.
