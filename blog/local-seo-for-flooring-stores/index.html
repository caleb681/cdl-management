Local SEO for flooring stores: a checklist from people who run three showrooms

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published October 30, 2026

Local SEO for a flooring store comes down to five things: a complete Google Business Profile, the same name, address and phone number everywhere you are listed, one website per store that you control, pages that answer real buying questions with your town in them, and a steady stream of reviews with replies. We run three Carpet One showrooms in Kirksville and Springfield, Missouri, and Peoria, Illinois, and this is the checklist we work from.

1. Google Business Profile

Your profile is what shows up in the map results, which is often the first thing a local shopper sees.

  • Primary category is the one that fits your store best (for most of us, "Flooring store"). Secondary categories match services you really sell.
  • Description is filled in, written in plain words, and names the towns you serve.
  • Services list every floor type you sell and install, in the words customers use. Our Peoria store went from 9 listed services to 22.
  • Website link uses https.
  • Regular hours are right, and holiday hours are set ahead of time.
  • A new post goes up every week.
  • Every review gets a reply.
  • Photos are real: your showroom, your storefront, your work.

The details of each step are in Google Business Profile for flooring stores: the setup we use.

2. Matching name, address and phone everywhere

Search engines check your store's details across many sites. When the listings disagree, it is harder for them to be sure which details are right.

  • Write down your exact store name, street address and main phone number once. That is the master copy.
  • Your website footer and contact page match it exactly.
  • Your Business Profile matches it.
  • Bing Places, Facebook, BBB and Houzz match it. At our stores, Bing Places is done for all three and we're still working through the rest.
  • Any old address or old phone number is gone from every listing you control.

3. One website per store, and you control it

On Oct 26 we moved our three stores off the corporate co-op websites and onto one website per store that we control. Before that, each store had two sites, and fixes had to be made twice or could not be made at all.

  • Each showroom has one main website, and it is the one your Business Profile links to.
  • Your ads point to that same site.
  • If you are moving from an old site, old page addresses redirect to the matching new page instead of a dead end.
  • You, or someone you trust, can add and edit pages without waiting on anyone.

If you are in a co-op, ask what you are allowed to change. Our co-op's corporate team helped us move the store web addresses over.

4. Schema on the homepage

Schema is code on your page that states facts like your business name, address, phone, hours and location in a format search engines read directly. We added it to every store homepage.

  • Your homepage has LocalBusiness schema (or a more specific type) with name, address, phone, hours and website.
  • The facts in the schema match your master copy exactly.
  • You do not mark up your own reviews as a star rating on your own site. Google does not allow that kind of self-serving review markup for local businesses.
  • Guide pages with an FAQ section carry FAQ schema.

5. Answer-first guide pages with the town in them

People search questions, not slogans. A page that answers one question clearly, for one town, gives search engines and AI tools something to quote.

At each of our store sites we added a Flooring Guides section in October. A real example: our Springfield store's vinyl plank installation guide.

  • Pick one real question your salespeople hear every week.
  • Put the floor type and the town in the title.
  • Answer the question in the first two sentences.
  • Use short sections and plain words for the details.
  • Only write what is true at your store. If you don't know a number, leave it out until you do.
  • End with three to five FAQs marked up with FAQ schema.
  • Link the page to your estimate or contact page, and link to it from a guides page.

Avoid copy-paste town pages that swap one town name for another. If you write about a town, say something true and specific about serving it.

6. Reviews and replies

  • Ask happy customers for a review, every job.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, in a normal human voice.
  • Never buy or fake reviews.

7. Measure it the right way

Rankings and clicks tell you whether more people are seeing you. They do not tell you whether you sold more floors.

  • Set up Google Search Console for each store site and look at clicks, impressions and average position once a month.
  • Give each channel its own phone number (Business Profile, website, each ad channel) so you know which one rang.
  • Log every call, grade it, and tie leads to sold jobs.
  • Judge your SEO work by sold jobs from organic search and your Business Profile over a full quarter, not by one good week.

8. Check AI search once a month

We ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers the same 17 questions every month, such as "best flooring store in Peoria IL," and score whether our stores get named.

  • Write 10 to 20 customer questions for your town.
  • Ask each AI tool each question, in a fresh chat.
  • Score each answer: named, mentioned or missing.
  • Fix one gap, then check again next month.

FAQ

How long does local SEO take for a flooring store?

Business Profile and listing fixes can show up in a few weeks, but rankings usually move over months, not days. Sold jobs from search take longer still, because flooring jobs take time to measure, quote and sell. Give any change at least a full quarter before you judge it, and judge it by sold jobs.

Do I need a separate page for every town I serve?

Not unless you have something true and useful to say about each one. A page that swaps one town name for another adds nothing. Start with answer-first guide pages for your main town, then add a town page when you can describe how you serve that town, what homes there are like and which services fit.

Can I do local SEO if my website belongs to a co-op?

You can still do most of it. Your Business Profile, directory listings and reviews are yours to manage. The website is the hard part, because you may not be able to add pages or fix problems. Ask your co-op what you are allowed to change. Ours helped us move to sites we control.

What is the most important local SEO task for a flooring store?

A complete Google Business Profile with every service you sell and install listed. It is free, it is what shows up in the map results, and services are easy to leave off. After that, make sure your name, address and phone number match on every listing.
