Google Ads for flooring stores: what we check in our own accounts

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 10, 2026

Google Ads can work for a flooring store when the account counts only real leads, keeps money off searches that will never buy from you, sends each click to the matching page on your own site and is judged by cost per sold job. Many of the problems we have found in our own accounts were settings, not budget.

This is the checklist we work through in the Google Ads accounts for the showrooms we operate. It is guidance to adapt to your store, not a report of measured client results.

Check what counts as a conversion

Google bids toward whatever the account calls a conversion, so start there. In Google Ads, open Goals, then Conversions, and look at which actions are marked primary. Primary actions steer bidding. Secondary actions are still recorded, but they do not.

Here is what we found in our own accounts:

  • A tap on the website call button, imported from analytics, was set as a primary conversion. In one account it made up most of the reported conversions, while actual calls and leads were a small share.
  • "Get directions" was one of the account's default conversion goals.
  • Actions imported from one store's analytics were counting as conversions in a different store's account.

We set each of those to secondary. The primary actions are now calls from ads and lead forms that actually reach our CRM. A tap or a directions request is a good sign, but it is not a customer yet.

Also check the call length on your call conversion. In one account we manage, real ad calls were coming in and none were recorded as conversions. The minimum call length was 60 seconds, and we shortened it to 30.

Read the search terms report every week

The keywords you choose and the searches you pay for are not the same thing. The search terms report shows what people actually typed before they clicked. In our flooring accounts, the money that bought nothing usually went to the same few kinds of searches:

  • Other flooring stores' names.
  • Big-box stores and warehouse clubs.
  • People shopping online or looking for a store directory.
  • How-to searches from people planning to do the job themselves.

We block those as phrase-match negative keywords at the campaign level. Before blocking a search, we check whether it ever brought a real lead. If it did, we leave it running.

Blocking waste is not the whole fix. After one round of negatives, the blocked searches stayed gone, but the campaign's cost per result did not improve right away. Most of the remaining clicks were general flooring questions. The next fix was the campaign itself, not more negatives.

Send each click to the matching page on your own site

Someone who searched for vinyl plank should land on your vinyl plank page, not your homepage. In October we moved our ads' final URLs from the homepage to the matching product pages, and we found sitelinks pointing to an old co-op site instead of ours.

Point your ads at a site you control, where you can see what visitors do and which number they call. What that site needs is in What a flooring store website should have: the checklist we use.

Give the ads their own phone number

The call number attached to your ads should be the tracked Google Ads number, not your main store line. Otherwise ad calls get counted as regular calls, and the campaign looks like it is getting nothing. Open each campaign and check which number is attached.

Then make sure that number rings through and someone answers it. A missed call from an ad is money already spent. The full setup is in How a flooring store should track phone calls: the setup we use.

Link the right Business Profile location

Link your Google Business Profile in each Google Ads account, so your ads can show your address and map location. If one profile manager holds several businesses, filter the link so only the right store shows. We found one account linked to a source with no locations in it, so its ads could not show the store's address at all. Keeping that profile accurate is covered in Google Business Profile for flooring stores.

Look at the account every day

A quick daily look catches problems before they cost a week of leads:

  • If a campaign spent nothing yesterday, check its status, budget and billing.
  • If it says Limited, read why. Limited by budget and limited by ad rank need different fixes, and raising the budget only helps the first.
  • If Google rates an ad Poor, write a new ad beside it, let the new one run, then pause the old one.

Judge it by cost per sold job

Every lead gets a source when it comes in, and every sold job keeps that source. Take what the campaign cost and divide it by the jobs it sold. That is the number we judge a campaign by, not cost per click or cost per conversion.

A campaign can look cheap per lead and still be expensive per sold job, because it rings the phone with people who were never going to buy. When a campaign has spend but no sold job tied to it yet, we write "can't measure." Not working, not failing. Unknown, until the data is there. We already send sold jobs from our CRM back to Meta so its ads learn from sales, and Google is next on that list.

How this fits with the rest of a store's marketing is in Marketing for flooring stores: a practical plan for showroom inquiries.

FAQ

Are Google Ads worth it for a flooring store?

They can be, if you judge them by sold jobs. Google Ads put your store in front of people searching for flooring right now. Whether that pays depends on what the account counts as a lead, which searches it pays for, where the clicks land and how many of those leads become sold jobs. Nobody can promise that in advance, so measure it.

Should a flooring store bid on competitor names?

In our own accounts we block other flooring stores' names, big-box stores and warehouse clubs after the search terms report shows them costing money without bringing leads. If one of those searches has brought a real lead, we leave it running and keep watching it.

What should count as a conversion in a flooring store's Google Ads account?

Primary conversions should be real leads: calls from ads that last long enough to be a conversation, and lead forms that reach the person who follows up. Taps on a call button, directions requests and page visits can stay as secondary actions, so they are still recorded but do not steer bidding.

How long should a flooring store test Google Ads before deciding?

Give a campaign at least a full quarter. Calls and forms show up right away, but sold jobs take longer because flooring jobs take time to measure, quote and sell. Until a campaign has sold jobs tied to it, call the result unknown rather than working or failing.
