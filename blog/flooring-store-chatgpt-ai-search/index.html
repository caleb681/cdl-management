How flooring stores can show up in ChatGPT and AI search

By Caleb Foglesong · October 30, 2026

Flooring stores show up in ChatGPT and other AI search answers when the plain facts about the store are written down clearly in public places these tools read: the store's own website, its Google Business Profile, its directory listings and its reviews. There is no trick or paid placement for it. We run three Carpet One showrooms in Kirksville and Springfield, Missouri, and Peoria, Illinois, and every month we check what ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers say about them.

Here is what we check, what we fix, and what we have not figured out yet.

What happens when someone asks an AI tool for a flooring store

When a shopper types "best flooring store in Peoria IL" into an AI tool, the tool writes an answer instead of handing back a list of links. To write it, the tool leans on what it can find and trust about local stores: websites, business listings, reviews and articles.

A store tends to get named when the tool can find clear, matching facts about it. What the store sells, where it is, which towns it serves, what customers say about it. A store with a thin website and a half-filled profile gives the tool very little to work with.

These answers also change. The same question can get a different answer from one day to the next, and from one tool to another. That is why one check tells you very little, and a monthly check tells you a lot more.

The monthly check we run

Every month we ask the same 17 questions about our markets to four tools: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers. The questions are the kind a real shopper asks, like "best flooring store in Peoria IL." Then we score each answer on whether our stores get named.

You can run your own version in about an hour.

  1. Write your questions. Ten to twenty is plenty. Mix the types:
    • "Best flooring store in [town]"
    • "Where can I buy luxury vinyl plank near [town]"
    • "Who installs carpet in [town]"
    • "Hardwood flooring store near [town]"
    • "Cabinet store in [town]," if you sell cabinets
  2. Ask every tool every question. Use a fresh chat or a private browser window so your own history does not color the answer.
  3. Score each answer the same way every time. We keep it simple: named, mentioned or missing. Write down which stores were named instead of yours.
  4. Save the answers. Copy and paste, or screenshot. You will want to compare next month.
  5. Look for the gap. Compare what the named stores have in public that you don't.
  6. Fix one thing, then check again next month.

What to fix first

These are the changes we made at our own stores. They are the same basics that help regular local search, which is part of the point. You are not building a second marketing plan for AI. You are making the facts about your store easy to find and easy to trust.

Your own website, under your control

On Oct 26 we moved our three stores off the corporate co-op websites and onto one website per store that we control. That let us add the pages and facts these tools look for, instead of being stuck with what a template allowed.

Answer-first guide pages

In October we added a Flooring Guides section to each store site. Each article answers one real question in its first two sentences, names the town, and ends with a short FAQ marked up with FAQ schema. Our Springfield store's vinyl plank installation guide is one example.

AI tools quote clear answers. A page that opens with a direct, two-sentence answer gives them a sentence to use. A page that opens with a slogan does not.

Schema on your homepage

We added schema to every store homepage. It states the store's name, address, phone and hours in a format machines read directly.

Matching listings everywhere

We're lining up the store name, address and phone number on Bing Places, Facebook, BBB and Houzz, starting with Bing Places, which is done for all three stores. Some AI tools draw on search engines other than Google, so claim and fix your Bing Places listing, not just your Google profile.

A complete Business Profile

Across nine Business Profiles we filled in full descriptions, services (our Peoria store went from 9 listed services to 22), https links and holiday hours, and we post weekly and reply to every review. Details are in Google Business Profile for flooring stores: the setup we use.

Reviews, with replies

Reviews are one of the few places where customers describe your store in their own words. Ask for them after every job and reply to every one.

What not to do

  • Don't claim "best" on your own site. Write facts that can be checked instead: what you sell, where you are, how your pricing works.
  • Don't stuff pages with town names. One clear page per real question beats ten pages that swap one town for another.
  • Don't buy or fake reviews.
  • Don't panic over one bad answer. Look at the trend over a few months.

What we don't know yet

We are not going to tell you AI search is replacing Google. We don't know that, and neither does anyone selling it as a sure thing. We also have not yet measured sold jobs from AI answers at our stores. Our scoreboard is sold jobs, and for AI search, that part is still unmeasured.

What we do know is that some shoppers are already asking these tools where to buy floors, the check costs nothing, and the fixes are the same ones that help your store in regular search. More on those in Local SEO for flooring stores: a checklist from people who run three showrooms.

FAQ

How do I get my flooring store to show up in ChatGPT?

Make the facts about your store easy to find and consistent everywhere: a website you control with answer-first pages that name your town, a complete Google Business Profile, matching listings on Bing Places, Facebook, BBB and Houzz, schema on your homepage, and steady reviews with replies. There is no paid placement. Check monthly to see what changes.

How often should I check what AI search says about my store?

Once a month is enough. AI answers can change from day to day, so a single check can mislead you. Ask the same questions to the same tools each month, score them the same way, and compare the trend over a few months before deciding whether a change helped.

Is AI search optimization different from regular SEO for a flooring store?

Mostly no. The basics are the same: a complete Business Profile, matching listings, schema, and pages that answer real questions with your town in them. The difference is emphasis. AI tools quote clear answers, so opening each page with a direct two-sentence answer matters even more.

Which AI tools should a flooring store check?

We check four: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers. They are widely used, and Google's AI answers appear right on the search results page many shoppers already use. Ask all four the same list of questions so the scores compare from month to month.
