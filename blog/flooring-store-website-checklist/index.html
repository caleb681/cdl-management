What a flooring store website should have: the checklist we use

By Caleb Foglesong · November 5, 2026

A flooring store website should have your address, hours and main phone number, real photos of your showroom, a plain answer on how pricing works, an easy way to call or text, and guide pages that answer the questions your salespeople hear every week. Its main job is to make a showroom visit feel easy, and it should be a site you control, so you can fix it when something is wrong. We run three Carpet One showrooms in Kirksville and Springfield, Missouri, and Peoria, Illinois, and this is the checklist we use on our own store sites.

Start with a site you control

On Oct 26 we moved our three stores off the corporate co-op websites and onto one website per store that we control. Before that, every store had two sites. Every fix had to be made twice, or it couldn't be made.

  • One main website per showroom, and it is the one your Business Profile links to.
  • Your ads point to that same site, so you can follow a customer from search to call.
  • Old page addresses redirect to the matching new page. When we moved, we set up redirects for 464 old corporate page addresses so nobody landed on a dead end.
  • You, or someone you trust, can add and edit pages without waiting on anyone.

If you are in a co-op, ask what you are allowed to change. Our co-op's corporate team helped us move the store web addresses over. The rest of the local search basics are in Local SEO for flooring stores: a checklist from people who run three showrooms.

Build the homepage around the visit, not a form

A lot of flooring websites are built around one button: "Get a free estimate." Flooring customers are already shopping. What they want to know is who they can trust, and whether the price will be fair.

On our store sites, the main asks are simple: call us, or have us text you the details, then come see it. The estimate form is still there. It just isn't the main event.

So the homepage has to make walking in feel easy:

  • Your address, hours and main phone number, the same as on your Business Profile and directory listings.
  • Real photos of your showroom and your work. No stock photos of people who don't work there.
  • What happens when someone comes in.
  • A straight answer on how pricing works. At our stores, that is one installed price with tear-out and haul-away included.
  • Your town in the page title and the opening lines, and the nearby towns you actually serve.

Put an FAQ on the homepage

In September we added an FAQ section to each of our three store homepages, built from the questions customers ask on the phone. Each one has matching FAQ schema, so search engines can read the questions and answers directly.

Add guide pages that answer real questions

Customers don't search for "about us." They search questions. How long does vinyl plank take to install? Do I have to move my furniture? Is laminate OK in a kitchen?

In October we added a Flooring Guides section to each of our store sites. Each page answers one real question in its first two sentences, puts the floor type and the town in the title, and ends with a short FAQ marked up with FAQ schema. One example is our Springfield store's vinyl plank installation guide.

  • Pick a question your salespeople heard this week, and write it down in the customer's words.
  • Answer it in the first two sentences, in plain words.
  • Only write what is true at your store. If you don't know a number, leave it out until you do.
  • Skip copy-paste town pages that swap one town name for another.

A page that opens with a direct answer also gives AI tools a sentence to quote. More on that in How flooring stores can show up in ChatGPT and AI search.

Get the phone number right

Every call button on your site should ring the store, and you should know which calls came from the website. At our stores, each website has its own tracked number on its call buttons. The main store number stays in the page code and in the footer next to the address, so it matches the Business Profile and listings.

In October we found two of our store sites showing the Google Ads number to every visitor. We fixed the buttons to show the website number unless the visitor came from an ad. The full setup is in How a flooring store should track phone calls: the setup we use.

Add schema to the homepage

Schema is code on your page that states your business name, address, phone and hours in a format search engines read directly. We added LocalBusiness schema to every store homepage.

  • The facts in the schema match your Business Profile exactly. How we set up the profile itself is in Google Business Profile for flooring stores: the setup we use.
  • Don't mark up your own reviews as a star rating on your own site. Google does not allow that kind of self-serving review markup for local businesses.

Check every page before you call it done

Template sites hide problems. On one of our old co-op store sites we found five live pages still showing placeholder text, including a kitchen page whose main heading read "General text area." On another store's site, the laminate page was titled "Backstop Laminate," with "Laminate Products Page" as its description.

Before a page goes live, and after any change:

  • Read each page title and description the way Google would show them. Each should say what the page is and name the town.
  • Look at the page on a phone. Check that nothing scrolls sideways, every image loads and every button is easy to tap.
  • Send a labeled test form and make sure it lands in your CRM or inbox.
  • Make a test call from the page and confirm it shows up under the right channel.

What we can't tell you yet

It is too early for us to tell you what our website switch did for sold jobs. When we have sold jobs to count against it, we will share them, good or bad. What we can tell you is that a site you control is the only kind you can fix.

FAQ

Does a flooring store in a co-op need its own website?

Not always, but find out what you can change. On our co-op sites we couldn't add the pages our customers needed, so we moved our three stores to one website per store that we control. Ask your co-op what you are allowed to change first.

Should a flooring store website have a free estimate form?

Yes, but it doesn't need to be the main button. On our store sites the main asks are to call, or have us text the details, and then come see the showroom. The estimate form is still there for people who want it.

What should a flooring store website say about pricing?

Give a plain explanation of how your pricing works and what it includes. At our stores, that is one installed price with tear-out and haul-away included. Only write what is true at your store.

How many guide pages does a flooring store website need?

Start with one. Pick the question your salespeople heard most this week, answer it in the first two sentences, and add a short FAQ. One good page this week beats the blog you're planning to start next year.
