Facebook Ads for Flooring Stores: What Works, What to Skip

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 18, 2026

Facebook and Instagram ads, which Meta sells from one account, work for flooring stores when they are used for the job they are good at: staying in front of local homeowners before they start shopping, and bringing back people who already visited your website. They rarely replace Google search ads for someone who needs a floor this week. Use real photos from your own store, keep the area local, give one clear next step, and judge the ads by calls, visits and sold jobs.

We run flooring showrooms ourselves and run these ads for them. This is how we think about it, not a report of client results.

What Facebook ads are good at, and what they are not

Facebook and Instagram adsGoogle search ads
Who sees themLocal homeowners who are not searching yet, and people who visited your sitePeople searching for flooring right now
Best forBeing the store they already know when they are readyCalls and visits this month
How long to judgeA few monthsA few weeks

If the budget only covers one, start with search. The search side is covered in Google Ads for flooring stores.

Who to show your ads to

  • People near your showroom. Set the area to the towns you really install in. A wide radius spends money on people who will never drive to you.
  • People who visited your website. This needs Meta's tracking code, called the pixel, on your site, and a clear notice in your privacy policy. These are your warmest audience.
  • Past customers. For care tips, new products and referrals, where you have their permission to market to them.
  • Everyone else nearby, chosen by Meta. Meta's system is good at finding likely buyers when the ad itself is clear about what you sell and where.

What to show

  • A room you just finished, with a before and after.
  • A short video walking through the showroom. Twenty seconds is enough.
  • A salesperson explaining how your pricing works, in plain words.
  • Your installers, by first name. People want to know who is coming into their home.
  • One product or one offer per ad, with your town in the first line.

Use your own photos, with permission where a customer's home is shown. Stock photos look like every other ad and tell the shopper nothing about your store.

Lead form or website?

Meta lets someone send you their details in a form without leaving Facebook or Instagram. It calls this an instant form, and the person's contact details may already be filled in.

  • Instant form. More leads, because it is easy. Also more people who tapped without much thought. Meta offers a "higher intent" form type that adds a review step to cut those down.
  • Your website. Fewer leads, and they have seen your store first. This only works if the page is fast on a phone and about the thing the ad showed.

Either way, call form leads the same hour. Someone who filled in a form on Facebook did not plan to shop today, and they cool off quickly. Meta requires a privacy policy link on these forms.

Product ads from your catalog

Meta can show people the exact floors they looked at on your site. This needs a product catalog and the pixel, and Meta lists price as a required field for each product. A store whose site says "call for price" cannot run these until it publishes prices.

If your ad mentions financing, read this first

Many flooring ads lead with a financing offer. Meta requires ads for financial products and services in the United States to be placed in a special ad category, and its examples include long-term financing and 0% APR installment payments. When that category applies, Meta limits targeting by age, gender and ZIP code and widens location targeting. An ad placed in the wrong category may be rejected.

So check before you build a campaign around "12 months same as cash." You may reach the right people more easily with an ad about the floor, and mention financing in the showroom and on your website.

How much to spend

There is no right number for every store. Two rules hold up:

  1. Spend an amount you can keep running for a few months. These ads work by being seen more than once.
  2. Judge it the way you judge everything else: what it cost divided by the jobs it sold.

The method for setting a target cost per sold job is in our flooring marketing guide.

How to tell if they are working

  • Give these ads their own phone number, so calls from them can be counted.
  • Ask every new customer how they heard about you, and write it down.
  • Record each lead with its source in your flooring CRM, and mark which ones became sold jobs.
  • Do not use likes, reach or Meta's "results" count as proof of sales. They are not sold jobs.

Six mistakes we see

  • Boosting a post with no goal and no next step.
  • A target area far wider than the store really serves.
  • Stock photos.
  • Form leads nobody calls for two days.
  • Judging the ads after one week.
  • A financing headline placed in the wrong ad category.

FAQ

Do Facebook ads work for flooring stores?

They can, for the right job. Facebook and Instagram ads are good at keeping a store in front of local homeowners before they shop and at bringing back people who already visited the website. They are weaker than Google search ads at reaching someone who needs a floor this week. Judge them by calls, visits and sold jobs over a few months.

Should a flooring store use Facebook ads or Google Ads?

Usually both, for different jobs. Google search ads reach people who are searching for flooring now. Facebook and Instagram ads reach homeowners who are not searching yet and remind the ones who have already looked at you. If the budget only covers one, start with search.

How much should a flooring store spend on Facebook ads?

Start with an amount you can keep running for a few months without strain, because these ads work by being seen repeatedly. Then judge it the same way as any channel: what it cost divided by the jobs it sold. Grow it while that number stays under what you can afford to pay for a sold job.

What kind of Facebook ad works best for a flooring store?

Real photos and short videos from your own showroom and finished jobs: a before and after, a room you just completed, a salesperson explaining how pricing works. One clear next step per ad. Stock photos and long lists of brands do worse.

Can a flooring store advertise financing on Facebook?

Yes, but check Meta's rules first. Meta requires ads for financial products and services, which its list says includes long-term financing and 0% APR installment payments, to be placed in a special ad category in the United States. That category limits targeting by age, gender and ZIP code.

Are Facebook ads and Meta ads the same thing?

Yes. Meta is the company that owns Facebook and Instagram, and one ad account runs ads on both. Most stores say Facebook ads. Meta calls them Meta ads.
