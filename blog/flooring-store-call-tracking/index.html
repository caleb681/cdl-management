How a flooring store should track phone calls: the setup we use

Practical guidance from the CDL Marketing team.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026

A flooring store should track phone calls by giving each marketing channel its own phone number, logging every call with the number it came in on, grading each call, and tying every lead to the sold job it becomes. Then you can say what each channel cost and how many floors it sold, instead of guessing at the counter. This guide draws on our hands-on flooring-industry experience.

Why a tracked call beats a conversion report

Ad platforms count almost anything as a conversion if you let them. We found one of our own store ad accounts counting taps on "get directions" as conversions. The report looked busy and could not tell us a single floor it sold.

A phone call from someone asking about carpet for three bedrooms is much closer to a sale. But it only helps you decide where to spend if you know which channel made the phone ring. If you can't tell which ad made your phone ring, you can't tell which ad sold a floor.

Give each channel its own number

Start with a separate phone number for each place you spend money or effort to get calls. All of them ring the store, so nothing changes for whoever answers.

  • One number for Google Ads.
  • One number for Local Services Ads.
  • One number for your website.
  • One number for any other ad channel you run.

Put each number only where it belongs. The ads number goes in the ads and nowhere else.

What about your Business Profile?

At our stores, the Business Profile carries the main store line, the same number as our directory listings. If you want profile calls counted separately, give the profile its own number as the primary number and list your main store number as an additional number. The details are in Google Business Profile for flooring stores: the setup we use.

Keep your main number the same everywhere else

Tracking numbers belong on channels. Your main store number belongs on your listings: Bing Places, Facebook, BBB, Houzz and the rest. Search engines compare your store's details across those sites, and listings that disagree make it harder for them to be sure which details are right. More on that in Local SEO for flooring stores: a checklist from people who run three showrooms.

Make sure your website shows the right number to the right visitor

In October we audited the phone numbers on every store website, ad account and listing. Two of our store sites were showing the Google Ads number to every visitor, including people who found the store in regular search. Those calls were being counted as ad calls, which made the ads look better than they were. We fixed it so each site's call buttons show the website number by default and switch to the ads number only for visitors who arrived from an ad.

Your main store number still stays in the page itself. It is the number in the site's code, in the business details search engines read, and in the footer next to your address, so it matches your Business Profile and listings. Only the call buttons change. If a script swaps every number on the page, search engines and directories can pick up a tracking number as your store's main number, and your listings stop agreeing with each other.

The one exception is a landing page that only your ads link to. Every visitor there came from an ad, so the ads number is right all the time.

Before we moved our stores onto websites we control, we found a different problem: the phone number on the old co-op store sites was not one of our tracked lines. Calls from those pages could not be counted at all. If your site belongs to a co-op or a vendor, find out whose number is on it.

After any change, check it yourself:

  • Open your site in a private browser window, without clicking an ad, and look at which number shows.
  • Tap the number on a phone and make sure it rings the store.
  • Make a test call and confirm it shows up in your call log under the right channel.

Log and grade every call

Every call gets logged with the number it came in on. Then it gets graded, because not every call is a lead.

  • A short wrong-number call is not a lead.
  • A vendor or an existing customer checking on an order is not a new lead.
  • Someone asking about carpet for three bedrooms is.

We get a daily email with the calls, so nobody has to dig for them. That email also lists strong sales calls that still have no lead in our CRM, so they get entered.

Tie every lead to a sold job

Every lead gets a source when it comes in, and every sold job keeps that source. Then you take what a channel cost and divide it by the jobs it sold. Cost per sold job is the number.

  • A channel can look cheap per lead and still be expensive per sold job, because it rings the phone with people who were never going to buy.
  • When a channel has spend but no sold job tied to it yet, we write "can't measure." Not working, not failing. Unknown, until the data is there.
  • Leads from Facebook lead ads go straight into our CRM, the same place as calls, with the same follow-up and source tracking.
  • We send sold jobs back to Meta, so its ads learn from sales instead of form fills.

A zero in the ad report is not always a problem

In one of our Google Ads campaigns, the ad's own call number showed zero impressions for about two weeks. The campaign was still getting calls. Google was showing the store's Business Profile location in the ad, and people called through that instead.

We left it that way. Our Business Profile keeps the main store number to match our listings, and our phone system logs every call either way. Before you change anything because a report shows zero, check where the calls are actually coming in.

We have also seen the opposite: a campaign with no call number of its own attached. Its calls rang the main line and were counted as regular calls, not ad calls, so the campaign looked like it was getting nothing. Check both ways. Open each campaign and make sure it has its own call number attached, then look at where the calls are actually landing.

FAQ

Can a single flooring showroom track calls this way?

Yes. A smaller version works fine for one showroom. You need two things: separate numbers that all ring the store, and a log of every call with the number it came in on. Start with one number for your ads and one for your website, and keep your main store line on your Business Profile and listings.

What counts as a lead from a phone call?

A call from someone asking about a real project, like carpet for three bedrooms, is a lead. A short wrong-number call is not, and neither is a vendor or an existing customer checking on an order. Grade every call so your lead counts reflect real buyers.

How do I check which phone number my website shows?

Open your site in a private browser window without clicking an ad and look at the call buttons. They should show your website number, and the footer with your address should show your main store number. Then tap a call button on a phone to make sure it rings the store, and make a test call to confirm it lands in your call log under the right channel.

How long before call tracking shows which channel works?

Calls show up right away, but sold jobs take longer, because flooring jobs take time to measure, quote and sell. Give a channel at least a full quarter of data, and judge it by cost per sold job rather than by how often the phone rang.
