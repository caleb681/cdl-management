Restoration company marketing: a plain guide for owners.

Practical guidance from a team that runs a restoration company.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 18, 2026

Restoration company marketing comes down to one moment: someone has water, smoke or mold in the house, searches on a phone and calls the first company that looks real. This guide covers what to fix first, where the calls come from and what we learned running our own restoration company.

Start with how your customer finds you

There are two kinds of restoration customer, and they need different marketing.

  • The emergency. A burst pipe, a flooded basement, a fire. They search, tap a call button and hire whoever answers. This is decided in minutes.
  • The planned job. Mold, a musty crawl space, smoke smell, carpet and duct cleaning. They read, compare and ask around for days.

Most wasted restoration marketing comes from treating the two the same.

Fix these first, in this order

  1. Answer the phone. Day and night, by a person. Every other step sends more calls to this one.
  2. Your Google Business Profile. Right categories, every service listed, real hours, fresh photos of your own trucks and crew.
  3. Your website on a phone. Number at the top as a button. Loads in a couple of seconds. A separate page for water, fire and mold.
  4. Call tracking. A number per source so you know what caused each call.
  5. Reviews. Ask every customer when the job is done. Reply to every one.

Then add paid placements

For emergency water jobs, the top of Google is paid. Local Services Ads come first, then search ads, then the map.

In our own restoration company, over one 30-day stretch every lead our Google accounts recorded was a phone call. In one market, 12 came from Local Services Ads and 5 from search ads. We also learned that a budget too small for the market buys nothing: with bids capped at $15 our ads barely showed, because Google’s first-page price there ran from $15.53 to $59.43 a click. Do not skip the old-fashioned sources

Plumbers, insurance agents, property managers and past customers send the best jobs many restoration companies get. No agency can build those relationships for you. What marketing can do is make you easy to recommend: a name people recognize, a site that looks trustworthy and reviews that back up the referral.

What we would not spend on

  • Facebook ads for water damage. We spent $1,412 and got zero form leads. The full numbers →
  • Giveaways. Cheap entries, not customers.
  • Fifty copied town pages. They do not rank.
  • A long contract. You should be able to leave any month.

How to judge any marketing, ours included

Count booked jobs, not clicks or impressions. For each source, know three numbers: what you spent, how many real calls came in and how many became jobs. If your agency cannot show you those, ask why.

Common questions

How do I market a restoration company?

Start with the Google Business Profile and a fast website with the phone number first. Add Local Services Ads and search ads for emergency water jobs, a steady review system, and referral relationships with plumbers and insurance agents. Track every call back to its source so you know what to keep.

What is the best advertising for water damage restoration?

Paid placements at the top of Google: Local Services Ads and search ads. Emergency customers call from there. In our own restoration company, Facebook ads did not sell water damage at all.

How much should a restoration company spend on marketing?

There is no honest single number. It depends on what clicks cost in your market, how many jobs your crew can take and what a job is worth to you. Work backwards: cost per call, calls per booked job, and what you can afford to pay for a job.

Does social media work for restoration companies?

Not for emergencies. It can work for planned services such as mold inspections, carpet cleaning and duct cleaning, and it helps people recognize your name later.
