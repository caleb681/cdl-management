How to get water damage leads without buying shared ones.

Practical guidance from a team that runs a restoration company.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 20, 2026

You can get water damage leads from six places: Local Services Ads, Google search ads, your Google listing, your own website, referrals and past customers. Bought lead lists are a seventh. The first six ring your own phone and build your own name. Here is what each costs and how to choose.

In our own restoration company’s small markets, Google’s first-page price on water searches ran from $15.53 to $59.43 a click. It takes more than one click to make a call, so work out your own cost per call before judging a month.

Buying leads: seven questions

Sellers of water damage leads rank at the top of Google for that phrase. Before you sign up, ask:

  1. Is each lead sold only to me?
  2. Is it a live phone call or a form?
  3. How old is it when I get it?
  4. Whose name was on the ad or page that produced it?
  5. What is refunded: wrong numbers, out of area, renters with no authority?
  6. Is there a minimum spend or a contract?
  7. What happens to my leads if I stop?

A bought lead can fill a slow week. But it builds the seller’s listing, reviews and rankings, never yours. The day you stop paying, you have nothing left.

What to do this week

  • Call your own number at 9 pm and see who answers.
  • Open your website on a phone on mobile data. Count the seconds until the call button shows.
  • Search "water damage restoration near me" from three places in your service area and see whether you are on the map.
  • Check that every ad and page has its own tracking number.

Common questions

What is the cheapest way to get water damage leads?

A complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of reviews and referrals from plumbers and insurance agents cost time rather than ad money. They build slowly. Ads bring calls sooner at a higher price.

Are exclusive water damage leads worth it?

An exclusive live call can be worth paying for. Check that exclusive really means sold to one company, that it is a live call and not an old form, and that there is a refund rule in writing.

How many clicks does it take to get a water damage call?

It depends on the page, the hour and the market, so measure it in your own account instead of trusting an average. That is why call tracking has to be in place before you judge a campaign.

Why are water damage clicks so expensive?

Because one job can be worth thousands of dollars, and franchises, lead sellers and local companies all bid on the same few searches. Google reports national average click prices of about $42 to $91 on emergency water searches.
