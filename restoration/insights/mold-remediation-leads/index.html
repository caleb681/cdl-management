Mold remediation leads: how to market a planned job.

Practical guidance from a team that runs a restoration company.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 18, 2026

Mold remediation leads come from people who are planning, not panicking. They search for days, read about health and cost, and compare companies. That makes mold cheaper to advertise than water damage, and it means the page, the ad and the offer all have to be different.

Mold is searched almost as often as water damage, and the clicks cost less. Many restoration companies treat it as an afterthought.

Give mold its own campaign and page

If mold shares a campaign with water, two things go wrong. Mold searches drain the water budget, and mold customers land on a page shouting about emergencies they do not have.

In our own restoration company’s accounts we block mold words in the water campaign. One detail matters: Google does not treat "mold", "mildew" and "mould" as the same word, so all three have to be blocked.

What a mold customer needs to see

  • An honest inspection. What you look at, what it costs or whether it is free, and what happens next. Do not advertise a free inspection unless you really offer one.
  • Plain answers. Is it dangerous, do I have to move out, will insurance pay, how long does it take.
  • No scare tactics. This customer has read a lot of frightening things already. Calm and specific wins.
  • Reviews that mention mold.
  • A form as well as a phone number. Unlike water customers, mold customers do fill in forms.

Where Facebook fits

Facebook did nothing for our water damage jobs, but it did work for mold. Mold inspection leads cost us $57.80 each there. People with a musty basement are not in a rush, and an ad can reach them before they search. All our Facebook numbers →

Mold travels better than water

A water job needs a truck there within the hour. A mold job can be booked for Thursday. So you can advertise mold across a wider area than emergency work without breaking a promise about response time.

Common questions

How do I get mold remediation leads?

Run mold as its own campaign with its own page. Mold customers research before they call, so they respond to a clear inspection offer, plain answers about cost and health, and reviews. Search ads, your Google listing and Facebook can all work for mold.

Do Facebook ads work for mold remediation?

In our own restoration company, yes, for inspections. Mold inspection leads from Facebook cost us $57.80 each, while water damage ads on Facebook produced no form leads at all.

Should I offer free mold inspections?

Only if you really do them free and can keep up with the requests. Do not advertise a free inspection you do not offer. An honest, clearly priced inspection also works, and it brings fewer people who were never going to buy.

Why keep mold and water in separate campaigns?

Different customer, different urgency, different click price. Mixed together, mold searches eat a water budget. Block mold, mildew and mould in the water campaign, because Google treats each as a different word.
