Facebook ads for restoration companies: what our own money taught us.

Practical guidance from a team that runs a restoration company.

By Caleb Foglesong · Published November 20, 2026

We have spent about $18,000 on Facebook ads for our own restoration company, across 11 campaigns. The short version: Facebook does not sell water damage, it does sell planned services such as mold inspections and cleaning, and two popular tactics made our results worse.

Why water damage failed

Nobody scrolling Facebook has a burst pipe at that moment, and the person who does is not on Facebook. They are on Google with a phone in a wet hand. An emergency service has to be where the emergency search happens: Local Services Ads, search ads and the map. How we get water damage calls instead →

Why planned services worked

Carpet cleaning, duct cleaning and mold inspections are things people have been meaning to do. An ad reminds them. They can fill in a form and book for next week. That is what Facebook is good at.

Two tactics that backfired

Narrowing the area

It feels sensible to show ads only to a tight circle around your town. On Facebook it roughly tripled our cost per lead. The audience got so small that Facebook charged more to reach it. Narrow targeting is right for Google search. On Facebook, give it room.

Giveaways

A giveaway produced "leads" at $2.13 to $6.24 each, which looks wonderful on a report. They were people entering a contest. Judge Facebook on booked jobs, never on cost per lead alone.

What we would do with a restoration Facebook budget

  • Spend nothing on emergency water or fire.
  • Run mold inspection and cleaning offers with a simple form.
  • Keep the audience wide across your whole service area.
  • Use real photos of your own crew and trucks.
  • Count booked jobs each month, and stop what does not book.

Common questions

Do Facebook ads work for water damage restoration?

Not in our experience. We spent $1,412 on two Facebook water damage campaigns for our own restoration company and got zero form leads. Thirteen calls were placed and two were confirmed. Emergency customers search Google. They are not scrolling Facebook.

What restoration services sell on Facebook?

Planned ones. In our own account, carpet cleaning leads cost $41.06, duct cleaning $48.82 and mold inspections $57.80.

Should I target a small area on Facebook?

Be careful. When we narrowed our Facebook ads to a tight local area, cost per lead roughly tripled: carpet cleaning went from $41.06 to $127.50 and duct cleaning from $48.82 to $140.62.

Are Facebook giveaways good for leads?

They are cheap and mostly worthless as leads. Ours cost $2.13 to $6.24 each, and the people were entering a contest, not buying a service.
