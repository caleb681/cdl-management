Water damage leads

Water damage leads that ring your phone and nobody else’s.

Google Ads and Local Services Ads built in your own accounts, by people who have paid for these clicks themselves.

The most reliable way to get water damage leads is to be at the top of Google when a pipe bursts: Local Services Ads first, search ads second, the map third. Those leads are calls to your own number. A bought lead is usually sold to several companies at once.

Why the lead is a phone call

Nobody fills in a form with water coming through the ceiling. In our own restoration company, over one 30-day stretch every lead our Google accounts recorded was a phone call. The website form recorded none.

So the whole setup has to serve the call: the number at the top of the page as a button, call tracking so you know which ad caused it, and someone who answers. An ad that rings a phone nobody picks up is the most expensive mistake in this trade.

Google Local Services Ads

These are the boxes at the very top of Google with a badge, your star rating and a call button. You pay per lead, not per click. To run them you go through Google’s checks on your license and insurance.

In one of our own markets, Local Services Ads brought 12 leads in a month and regular search ads brought 5. What moves you up is mostly reviews, how close you are, and how quickly you answer. Miss the calls and Google shows you less.

Search ads let you choose the exact searches, towns and hours. They are also where restoration money gets burned, because the clicks cost so much.

Even in our own small markets, Google’s first-page price on water damage searches ran from $15.53 to $59.43 a click. When our bids were capped at $15, the ads barely showed. Raising the cap to $30 is what got them seen. A budget that is too small for your market does not buy cheap leads. It buys none.

Where the money leaks

These all came out of our own accounts.

  • Paying for your own name. In one water damage campaign, about 83% of the spend was going to people searching our company name, at up to $26 a click. They would have found us anyway. We blocked our name in that campaign.
  • The wrong location setting. Google’s default shows ads to people "interested in" your area, not only people in it. We switched every campaign to people physically there.
  • Desktop clicks. In one campaign, desktop cost about $300 in a month at about $23 a click and produced no recorded leads, three periods in a row. We cut desktop bids by 80%.
  • Mold clicks in a water campaign. Blocking "mold" is not enough. Google treats "mildew" and "mould" as different words, so each has to be blocked.
  • No tracking. We found a booking page with no ad tracking on it at all, so the ads sending people there could not report one booking. If you cannot see which ad made the phone ring, you cannot fix anything.

Buying leads

Lead sellers rank well for "water damage leads" because that is their business. Some are fine as a top-up. Before you pay, ask:

  • Is the lead sold to me only, or to several companies?
  • Is it a live call, or a form filled in an hour ago?
  • How was it generated, and under whose name?
  • What is the refund rule for wrong numbers and out-of-area calls?
  • Can I stop any month?

How do I get water damage leads?

The steady sources are Google Local Services Ads, Google search ads, your Google Business Profile, your own website, plumber and insurance agent referrals, and past customers. Paid lead lists are a seventh source, and usually the most expensive per booked job because the same lead is sold to several companies.

How much does a water damage lead cost?

It varies a lot by market. Nationally, Google reports average click prices of about $42 to $91 on emergency water searches, and it takes several clicks to make one call. In our own small markets, Google’s first-page price ran from $15.53 to $59.43 a click. We work out a realistic cost per call for your town before you spend.

Should I buy water damage leads?

Sometimes it makes sense as a top-up, but ask first whether the lead is exclusive, how old it is, how it was generated and what the refund rule is. A lead generated in your own account is yours alone and builds your own listing and reviews. A bought lead builds the seller’s.

Are Local Services Ads better than Google Ads?

They do different jobs and most restoration companies should run both. Local Services Ads sit at the very top, charge per lead and lean heavily on your reviews and how fast you answer. Search ads give you control over the exact searches, towns and hours. In one of our own markets, Local Services Ads brought 12 leads in a month and search ads brought 5.

Do you sell leads or use shared call numbers?

No. The ads run in your own Google accounts and the calls ring your own phone. We add a tracking number so you can see which ad caused each call, and that number is yours.
