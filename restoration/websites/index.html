Restoration websites

A restoration company website built for someone standing in water.

Fast pages, the phone number first, and a separate page for water, fire and mold. Built by people who run a restoration company.

A restoration company website has one main job: turn a worried visitor on a phone into a call within seconds. That means the number at the top as a button, a page that loads fast, plain proof that you are a real local company, and a separate page for each service.

The first screen

Most visitors decide before they scroll. On our own restoration pages the call button sits directly under the headline, and a call bar stays on screen as the visitor moves down the page. The first screen should answer three questions without a single tap:

  • Do you do what I need?
  • Do you come to my town?
  • How do I reach a person right now?

One page per service

A water damage customer and a fire customer are in different places. Our fire pages are written around the hours after the fire department leaves: board-up, securing the house, what to keep and what not to touch. Our mold pages are written around an honest inspection, because that customer is deciding who to trust, not racing a clock.

Separate pages also let each ad land on the right message, which lowers what you pay per call.

Proof you are real

The visitor is about to let a stranger into a damaged home. Show them:

  • Your real Google rating and a few real reviews, used with permission.
  • Photos of your own crew, trucks and equipment.
  • The certifications and licenses you actually hold.
  • How long you have been doing this, if it is true and you can back it up.
  • The towns you serve, by name.

Do not invent any of it. One wrong date or fake badge undoes the rest.

Speed

A paid water damage click often costs $40 or more. If the page takes six seconds to appear on a phone, part of that money leaves before your headline shows. We load fonts and pictures so the text and the call button appear at once, even on a weak signal in a basement.

Tracking

Every call button and every form reports back to the ad that caused it. That sounds basic, and it is often missing. We have found it missing on a page of our own. Without it, every decision about the ad budget is a guess.

Common questions

Restoration websites
questions.

What should a restoration company website have?

The phone number at the top of every page as a button, a clear list of what you do and where, proof that you are real (reviews, photos of your own crew and trucks, licenses and certifications you actually hold), a page for each service, and a short form for people who are not in an emergency.

Do I need a new website?

Not always. If your current site loads fast on a phone, shows the number first and you can edit it, we can work with it. We check that in the free audit before recommending a rebuild.

Why does speed matter so much?

Because the visitor is often standing in water. If the page takes six seconds to load on a phone, they go back and tap the next company. Every paid click that leaves before the page loads is money spent on nothing.

Who owns the website?

You do. Your domain and your accounts stay in your name. If you stop working with us, you keep the site.

Can you use stock photos?

We would rather not. Real photos of your crew, trucks and equipment do more to prove you are a real local company than any stock picture. A phone photo is fine. We never use a customer’s home or name without their permission.
