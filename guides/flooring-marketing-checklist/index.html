For showroom owners

A practical flooring
marketing checklist.

Six checks to help you see what works, what needs attention and what you still need to find out.

Use this flooring marketing checklist to turn a broad concern into specific questions about your website, local visibility and advertising. Your observations help start a conversation; this page does not scan your site or calculate a marketing score.

Start with what you can see

Use this with your own team, current provider or CDL. No login or email is needed. Review public pages and accounts you already have permission to use. Mark unknown items honestly. Avoid changing live settings or submitting real inquiries during this first look.

Your choices are used only on this page. This checklist does not send or store them. Use your browser’s print option to keep a copy; they are not attached to a contact request.

Begin the six checks ↓

1. Can a homeowner take the next step?

Open your homepage and one important flooring or installation page on your phone. Can you find the location, the help you offer and a clear way to ask about an estimate? Check the destination shown by phone, email and form links without sending a test inquiry.

What to do with what you find

Write down the page and the point where a shopper could get stuck. Agree a controlled delivery test with the receiving team before treating a form as verified.

Website and inquiry path

2. Do your local business details agree?

Compare your public Business Profile with your website: real business name, address, phone, regular and special hours, and the area you actually serve. Note missing or conflicting facts. One search on your own phone does not establish your local ranking.

What to do with what you find

Confirm the correct details with your team, then identify who has permission to make each correction.

Local business information

3. Do your pages answer real flooring questions?

Choose a product or installation service you actually offer. Look for a useful page explaining it, where it is available and how to get help. Use accurate descriptions and real photos you have permission to use. List questions the page leaves unanswered.

What to do with what you find

Choose one important unanswered shopper question and the existing page that should address it before planning more pages.

Useful pages for search and AI

4. Does an ad lead to the right page?

If you run ads, inspect the saved ad and its destination through an account you are authorized to use. Does the product, offer, location and next step agree with the landing page? Check the intended area and approved budget. Do not click your live ads to test them.

What to do with what you find

Record the specific mismatch and ask the account owner to review it. Keep management fees, platform media and optional tools separate when comparing costs.

Advertising and landing pages

5. Do the numbers describe useful inquiries?

Ask which actions count as a conversion: a form, a call, a directions click or a purchase. Review definitions and date ranges before comparing totals. Ask who answers new inquiries and how you learn whether they became estimates or sold jobs. Use summaries, not customer records, for an initial conversation.

What to do with what you find

Agree one useful inquiry definition, a responsible person and a review period. Record missing tracking rather than counting every platform conversion as a sale.

Measurement and response

6. Do you know what can stay and what can move?

List your domain, website, business email, Business Profile, ad accounts, catalogs and other licensed tools. Note the owner or administrator and what you do not yet know. Review the current agreement privately for notice, renewal and access or export limits. A new website is not automatically necessary.

What to do with what you find

Confirm access, rights and dependencies before choosing a replacement or cancellation date. Keep business email and inquiry destinations in the transition plan.
