Switch readiness

Know what can move.
Protect what must work.

A practical checklist for changing website providers around your actual agreement, accounts and customer paths.

Switching flooring website providers starts with knowing what must keep working. Use this checklist with your team and providers before choosing a move date. Your existing site may support the marketing work you need and can stay where it is.

Keep a private evidence record

For each check, note the item, status, supporting record, date, reviewer and next responsible person. Use confirmed only when the reviewer checked evidence. Owner-reported means someone supplied the information without verification; unknown means evidence is missing; conflicted means records disagree. Mark not needed only with a reason.

This checklist has no entry fields or uploads and does not clear a launch. Keep agreements, credentials and customer records out of public inquiry forms.

1. Read the actual agreement

Find the signed agreement, amendments and applicable service terms. Record renewal, notice deadline and service-end date separately. Identify how notice must be delivered, who may give it and what acknowledgement is needed. Do not assume another retailer’s terms apply to you.

Evidence: the relevant clauses, document version and any written clarification. Identify outstanding payments, export limits and services that end together. Action: resolve conflicting dates before setting a move date or sending cancellation. A proposed CDL credit does not change your current obligations.

If a notice deadline may be close, ask the authorized owner or adviser to review it promptly. Raise that question before waiting for the whole technical move plan.

A useful question for your provider

Which document establishes each date, and who will confirm receipt if notice is sent?

2. Separate ownership from access

Identify the domain’s registrant, the registrar managing its registration, the DNS service directing traffic, the website host and the business-email provider. These may be different companies. Confirm who controls each account and its recovery method. An agency login alone does not establish retailer ownership.

Evidence: current account records and platform-native owner or administrator roles for your Business Profile, search, analytics and advertising accounts. Record agency access separately. Action: have the authorized owner arrange appropriate invitations and recovery access. Map email records before DNS changes; website hosting and business email may be handled separately.

A useful question for your provider

Can our own authorized administrator confirm control without relying on the outgoing agency’s login?

3. Check what can move

List important page addresses, text, original photos, downloadable files and product catalogs. Include room visualizers, appointment tools and other licensed services. Owning your domain does not establish rights to every image, design, product feed or tool on the site.

Evidence: licenses, written permissions, export documentation and a sample export inspected for completeness. Action: decide what stays, can be reused, needs permission or needs replacing. Confirm catalog access, fees and compatibility individually. Do not assume an export recreates the website or transfers a tool. Preserve useful URLs; map changed addresses to suitable replacements and plan redirects.

A useful question for your provider

Can we inspect an export and its reuse permissions before promising to retain this feature?

4. Preserve a useful baseline

Record the correct analytics property, search property, ad accounts and installed tracking tags. Save comparable date ranges and definitions for forms, calls and other conversions. Platform conversions are not automatically estimates or sold jobs. Record missing measurement as unknown, rather than zero.

Evidence: dated reports, account identifiers, access roles and documented tag behavior. Action: agree which properties continue, how consent choices work and who checks duplicate tags or events. Preserve the baseline before changes. A candidate-site test cannot prove continuity in the live account; arrange that verification separately with authorized access.

A useful question for your provider

Which changes could make reports look better while counting the same inquiry twice?

5. Protect every inquiry route

List public phone numbers, call tracking, email links, forms, booking tools and their intended destinations. Confirm which phone numbers can be retained and who controls them. Name the person who receives each route and the backup. A visible thank-you message alone does not prove an inquiry was saved, delivered or assigned.

Evidence: route configuration and a separately authorized, coordinated test record showing receipt, destination and receiving-person acknowledgement. Action: first inspect links and settings without submitting. Agree test details, recipients and cleanup before any delivery test. Do not send real public submissions as part of this checklist. Retain existing inquiry records securely and keep a working contact route throughout the move.

A useful question for your provider

Who will confirm both the saved test receipt and its arrival with the right person?

6. Stage, verify and keep a way back

Review the candidate site before changing live traffic. Check mobile pages, important URLs, redirects, contact details, approved content and licensed tools. A hosting-only move keeping the same addresses differs from a domain or URL change. Search visibility can fluctuate during a move.

Evidence: candidate version, completed checks, restorable backups and previous routing settings. Action: name the change owner and approver, choose a window and agree reversible steps. Set rollback triggers, such as unavailable key pages, broken email or failed inquiry delivery, and who can restore service. Verify live pages, email and inquiry routes after the authorized cutover. Preserve inquiries first. Close the old service only after agreed checks, recovery needs and contractual obligations are handled.

A useful question for your provider

If a critical route fails, who restores the previous setup and checks new inquiries?

Choose one next action

Start with the first unresolved dependency, assign a person and set a review date. Keeping the current site, correcting access or requesting a missing document can be a useful outcome. Do not cancel service because the checklist looks complete.

Review proposed CDL switching credits separately. Eligibility, scope and commercial terms need written agreement; this guide starts no paid service or credit.

Official guidance for your provider review

  • ICANN: domain transfers and hosting changes
  • Cloudflare: website and mail DNS records
  • Google: Business Profile owners and managers
  • Google: Analytics access and permissions
  • Google: site moves with URL changes
