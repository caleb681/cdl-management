Buyer’s guide

Choose an agency
with clear answers.

A practical checklist for independent flooring showroom owners.

By CDL Marketing · November 4, 2026

Choose a flooring marketing agency by the work it will do, the evidence behind its recommendations and the terms you can review. Start with your showroom's problem: getting found, making the website useful or reaching more local shoppers.

Eight questions before you hire

  1. What will you do for my showroom?

    Ask for specific deliverables, locations covered, responsibilities and exclusions. Separate local SEO, ads and website work. A broad service label is not a work plan.

  2. Do you understand how flooring is sold?

    Discuss products, service areas, installation questions and how customers request a measure. Ask for relevant experience. Our showroom operating background informs our approach, but experience alone does not prove client results.

  3. What does the full bill include?

    Request separate lines for platform or hosting fees, agency management, setup, content and advertising spend. Ask what changes the price and which optional tools cost extra. Compare the same scope across proposals.

  4. What proof can you show?

    Ask for examples with dates, starting conditions, work completed and spend context. Check whether a result means inquiries, booked measures, sold jobs or collected revenue. A percentage without a baseline can hide more than it explains.

  5. Who controls the website and accounts?

    Confirm domain registration, hosting, content rights, administrator access and control of ad and analytics accounts. Put cancellation, export, handover and any transfer charges in writing before signing.

  6. What access will you need?

    Agree on website, business-profile and advertising permissions. If CRM access or integration is proposed, identify the supported connection, data involved, owner and testing plan. Ask what happens if that connection is unavailable.

  7. How will we know what is working?

    Agree on a qualified inquiry and which outcomes you can reliably record. Ask to see a sample report that explains findings, missing data and next actions. Calls and forms should not automatically be counted as sales.

  8. What can you reasonably promise?

    Ask what the agency controls and what depends on your market, access and staff. Be cautious about guaranteed rankings or AI recommendations. AI search work should have a concrete scope, just like other marketing.

A practical first 90 days

Use this as a review framework, with timing agreed around access and scope:

  • First month: establish the starting picture, confirm access and measurement, agree priorities and fix the most useful basics.
  • Second month: carry out the agreed campaigns or content work and check the contact paths. Review actual inquiry quality with your team.
  • Third month: compare results with the starting picture, explain limitations and decide what to improve next. Organic visibility may need sustained work beyond this period.

Leave with something you can review
