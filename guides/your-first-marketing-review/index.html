Your first review

Start with your showroom.
Agree a useful next step.

A guide to the first assessment, the work you might need and a careful transition if you choose to change providers.

The first marketing review starts with the showroom you have today: what you sell, where you serve customers, how people ask for help and what you want to improve. The aim is to identify three useful priorities and decide what is worth investigating next.

Bring a few facts, not a folder of passwords

  • Your showroom name, location, public website and the products or installation services you want to discuss.
  • One specific concern, such as a confusing estimate page, incorrect hours or an ad leading to the wrong product.
  • What you know about the current provider, who controls the domain and accounts, and any known renewal or end date. “I need to check” is a useful answer.
  • If available, a summary of recent inquiries and ad spend for the same date range, with an explanation of what each number counts. A report is optional for the first conversation.

Keep customer records, account credentials and full agreements out of the public inquiry form. If an account-level review is needed later, agree the purpose and limited access with the account owner. Use access invitations rather than sharing passwords.

Use the six-check worksheet to organize observations. Its answers stay on that page and are not sent to CDL.

What the first assessment can establish

  1. Understand the showroom. Confirm the work you actually offer, service area, current site and the problem you want solved.
  2. Review what is visible. Look at public pages, local information and the route to an inquiry. Record specific observations and anything that needs account access or a controlled test.
  3. Discuss three priorities. Separate an observed issue from a hypothesis. Decide what can be improved on the current setup and what needs investigation before a proposal.

A public-page review cannot establish every account setting, form destination, sales result or contractual right. We identify those limits. Asking for a review starts no paid service or switching credit.

Plan around the website you already have

You do not need to cancel your current provider to start a conversation. Where access, compatibility and the inquiry path support the agreed work, SEO or advertising can use your existing website.

  1. Map what must keep working. List the domain, business email, phone routing, forms, important page URLs, Business Profile, ad accounts, catalogs and licensed tools. Confirm each owner, access limit and relevant agreement.
  2. Agree the actual change. Decide what stays, what changes, who approves it and who makes the cutover. Review rights, notice and any proposed CDL credits separately. Do not assume a catalog, design or license can transfer.
  3. Prepare and verify. Keep useful URLs when possible. If URLs must change, prepare old-to-new mappings and appropriate redirects. Check the candidate pages on mobile and desktop, and arrange controlled inquiry-delivery checks with the receiving team.
  4. Switch deliberately, then monitor. Confirm backups, recovery steps, ownership and a suitable change window before DNS or other live changes. Monitor important pages and inquiry routes afterward. Keep the old service until the agreed transition and existing obligations are handled.

A domain or URL change is different from changing hosting while keeping the same URLs. Search visibility can fluctuate during a move. The plan should fit the actual change, not assume a redesign will protect or improve every ranking.

Work through the switch-readiness checklist for evidence, unknowns and the cutover plan.

Review the proposed switching support and open terms. Credits concern CDL website-service fees; they do not pay your old provider or cancel its agreement.

Leave with one clear next step

Write down the observation, the evidence still needed, the responsible person and the next review point. A useful outcome may be a specific correction, an access check, an itemized proposal or a decision to keep things as they are. You can decide whether to proceed after reviewing it.

Before paid work, agree scope, responsibilities, separate fees/media/tools, timing, account control and any notice or transition terms in writing. No ranking, AI mention, lead count or sales result is promised.
