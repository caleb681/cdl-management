Flooring marketing: a practical guide for independent flooring stores

By Caleb Foglesong · October 30, 2026

Flooring marketing works when four things line up: people nearby can find you on Google, your website loads fast on a phone and makes a visit feel easy, ads reach shoppers who are ready now, and every call and form is traced to a sold job. Work on them in that order. Most stores do not need more tactics. They need the basics finished and measured.

We run flooring showrooms ourselves, and this guide to flooring store marketing is the order we work in. It is guidance to adapt to your store, not a report of client results.

The short version

  1. Finish your Google Business Profile. It is free and it is where most local shoppers meet you first.
  2. Fix the website basics. Fast on a phone, real photos, plain pricing, an easy way to call or visit.
  3. Do the local SEO work. Pages for what you sell and where you sell it, and matching business details everywhere.
  4. Ask for reviews every week. Honestly, with no reward attached.
  5. Add Google Ads for demand that exists today. Then Facebook and Instagram to stay in front of homeowners.
  6. Answer and follow up. Marketing that reaches a phone nobody answers is wasted.
  7. Track sold jobs by source. Keep what pays. Cut what does not.

1. Finish your Google Business Profile

When someone searches "flooring store near me," Google shows a map with a short list of businesses, often before any website. Your profile decides whether you are on that list and what the shopper sees.

  • Pick the category that matches what you mainly are, and add the products and services you really sell.
  • Keep hours right, including holidays.
  • Add current photos of the showroom, the team and finished jobs you have permission to show.
  • Link to your own website, and to an estimate or contact page that works on a phone.
  • Reply to reviews, good and bad, in your own words.

Google's profile-editing guide covers how to make changes. Our Google Business Profile checklist for flooring stores goes line by line.

2. Fix the website basics, starting with speed

Your website has one job: make the next step easy. For most flooring shoppers that step is a visit or a call, not a form.

Speed comes first, because nothing else on the page matters if the shopper leaves before it loads. In November 2026 we tested 831 independent flooring store websites with Google's phone speed test. The median score was 66 out of 100, and only one site in ten scored in the range Google calls good. The typical home page took 6.6 seconds to show its main content. If your site is quick, you are already ahead of most of the stores we tested.

After speed, check that the home page answers five questions without scrolling far:

  • Who are you, and where is the showroom?
  • What do you sell and install?
  • How does pricing work?
  • What happens when I come in?
  • How do I call, text or get directions right now?

Use real photos. Shoppers can tell a stock photo from your store. The full list is in what a flooring store website should have. If the site itself is the problem, see our flooring website design service.

3. Do the local SEO work

Local SEO is the work that helps you show up when someone nearby searches for what you sell. Three things carry most of it:

  • A page for each main product and service. Carpet, luxury vinyl plank, hardwood, tile, installation. Say what you carry and how the process works at your store.
  • Your towns. Name the towns you serve in plain sentences, on the pages where it is true.
  • The same name, address and phone number everywhere. Website, Business Profile, Facebook and directories should match.

Write pages that answer the questions your salespeople hear. One useful page a month is better than a blog nobody keeps up. See the local SEO checklist for flooring stores, or our flooring store SEO service.

4. Ask for reviews every week

Reviews help you get chosen once you are found. Make asking a habit tied to something that already happens, such as the final walk-through or the paid invoice.

  • Ask in person, then send the link by text the same day.
  • Ask every customer, not only the happy ones.
  • Never offer a discount or gift for a review. Google prohibits incentives for posting, changing or removing reviews.
  • Reply to each one within a few days.

5. Add Google Ads for shoppers who are ready now

Search ads reach people who typed in what you sell today. They are the fastest way to add calls, and the easiest place to waste money.

  • Start with searches that show buying intent in your area, such as "carpet installation" plus your town.
  • Block searches that never buy: jobs, DIY how-to, free samples, and brands or services you do not carry.
  • Send each ad to a page about that product, not the home page.
  • Give ads their own phone number so you know which calls came from them.
  • Count a lead only when a real person asked about a real project.

What we check in our own accounts is in Google Ads for flooring stores. If you want it run for you, see Google and Facebook ads for flooring stores.

6. Use Facebook and Instagram for a different job

People do not open Facebook to buy flooring. Ads there do two things well: they keep your store in front of local homeowners before they start shopping, and they remind people who already visited your website. Show real rooms and real people from your store. Judge these ads by calls, visits and requests over a few months, not by likes.

7. Make your facts easy for AI search to read

Some shoppers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI answers where to buy flooring. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in its AI features beyond good search basics. So this is not a separate trick. State who you are, where you are, what you sell and how pricing works in plain sentences, keep those facts the same everywhere, and answer real questions on your site. More in how flooring stores can show up in AI search.

8. Answer the phone and follow up

A lot of marketing money is lost here, and no agency can fix it from the outside.

  • Answer during posted hours. Return missed calls the same day.
  • Reply to website requests within the hour when you are open.
  • After an estimate, follow up in two days and again in a week.
  • Write down how each new customer heard about you, every time.

9. Track sold jobs, not clicks

A click, a call, an estimate and a sold job are four different things. Reports from ad platforms count the first two. Your bank account counts the last.

  • Use a separate phone number for each main source, such as ads and your website.
  • Log every lead with its source.
  • Mark which leads became sold jobs.
  • Each month, divide what you spent on a source by the jobs it sold.

Twelve flooring store marketing and advertising ideas to start this month

  1. Photograph every finished job you have permission to show, and post the best one each week on your Business Profile.
  2. Write down the ten questions your salespeople hear most. Turn them into an FAQ on your home page.
  3. Add a short page for each town you install in, with real jobs from that town.
  4. Hand every customer a card with a review link at the final walk-through.
  5. Put your installers on the website by first name with a photo. People want to know who is coming into their home.
  6. Publish how your pricing works, even if you cannot publish every price.
  7. Run your home page through Google's phone speed test and fix the top photo first.
  8. Email past customers once a season with one useful thing, such as care tips or what is new in the showroom.
  9. Introduce yourself to three local builders, remodelers or real estate agents each month.
  10. Put a yard sign at each job, with permission, for the week of the install.
  11. Add a tracked phone number to your ads so you can see which calls they cause.
  12. Search your own store on a phone from a nearby town and fix the first thing that looks wrong.

How much should a flooring store spend on marketing?

Skip the rules of thumb. Work it out from your own jobs.

  1. Find your average gross profit on a sold job.
  2. Decide what share of that profit you are willing to spend to win one more job.
  3. That figure is your target cost per sold job.
  4. Each month, compare every channel to that target.

Here is the math with made-up numbers. If a store averages $1,500 in gross profit on a job and is willing to spend one fifth of it to win a job, its target is $300 per sold job. A channel that costs $900 in a month and sells four jobs is at $225 each and worth growing. A channel that costs $900 and sells one job is not. Your numbers will be different. The method is the point.

How long does it take?

Ads can bring calls within days once they are set up properly. Search work is slower. Google's own starter guide says some changes take effect in a few hours and others can take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks to judge a change. Reviews build one customer at a time. Nobody can honestly promise you a ranking or a number of leads by a date.

Do it yourself or hire help?

Most of this guide can be done by an owner or manager with a few hours a week. Hire help when the work is not getting done, or when you cannot tell which marketing is producing sold jobs. If you hire, keep ownership of your website, domain, ad accounts and Business Profile, and get the scope, fees and cancellation notice in writing. Five mistakes we see most

  • A home page built around one "free estimate" button, when many shoppers want to visit or call.
  • Stock photos in place of the real showroom.
  • Ads that send everyone to the home page.
  • Judging marketing by clicks and impressions.
  • Paying for more leads while calls go unanswered.

FAQ

What is the best marketing for a flooring store?

Start with the free and owned basics: a complete Google Business Profile, a website that loads fast on a phone and makes visiting easy, and a steady way to ask happy customers for reviews. Then add Google Ads for shoppers who are ready now. Judge all of it by sold jobs, not clicks.

How do I get more customers for my flooring store?

Make it easy for people nearby who are already shopping to find you and take the next step. That means showing up on Google Maps for the products you sell, a home page that answers who you are, where you are and how pricing works, and answering or returning every call the same day.

How much should a flooring store spend on marketing?

Work it out from your own numbers instead of a rule of thumb. Take your average gross profit on a job and decide how much of it you are willing to spend to win one more job. That is your target cost per sold job. Spend more on a channel while it stays under that target and cut it when it does not.

Do Facebook ads work for flooring stores?

They can, for a different job than Google Ads. Google reaches people searching for flooring today. Facebook and Instagram reach homeowners before they search, and remind people who already visited your site. Use real photos of your showroom and finished work, and count calls and visits, not likes.

How long does flooring store marketing take to work?

Ads can bring calls within days of a correct setup. Search work is slower. Google says some changes take effect in a few hours and others can take several months. Plan on a few weeks to see whether a change helped, and a few months for local search and reviews to build.

How do I advertise a flooring business?

Start where people are already looking: a complete Google Business Profile and search ads for the products you sell in your own town. Add Facebook and Instagram to stay known locally. Give your ads their own phone number so you can see which calls they caused.

How do I get more flooring leads?

Most stores get more leads by fixing what loses them: a slow website, a home page with no clear next step, missed calls and unanswered web requests. After that, search ads aimed at buying searches in your area add leads fastest. Count a lead only when a real person asked about a real project.

Should a flooring store hire a marketing agency?

Hire one when the work is not getting done, or when you cannot tell which marketing produces sold jobs. Ask to keep ownership of your website, domain, ad accounts and Business Profile, ask for month-to-month terms in writing, and ask how results will be tied to sold jobs.
