Flooring Leads: How to Get More Without Buying Shared Leads

A flooring store gets more leads in two ways: by keeping the ones it already loses, and by reaching more people who are shopping now. Start with the first. A slow website, a home page with no clear next step and calls nobody returns cost most stores more leads than any ad can replace. Then add search ads, local SEO and reviews, and count a lead only when a real person asks about a real project.

We run flooring showrooms ourselves. This is how we think about leads in our own stores, not a report of client results.

What counts as a flooring lead

Agree on this before you count anything. A lead is a real person asking about a real project: carpet for three bedrooms, a quote on vinyl plank for a kitchen, a measure for a hardwood refinish. These are not leads:

  • Wrong numbers and hang-ups
  • Vendors and job seekers
  • Existing customers checking on an order
  • A click, a page view or a "direction request" with no contact

If your reports count those, your cost per lead looks better than it is.

Step 1: stop losing the leads you already get

These are the cheapest leads you will ever add, because you have already paid for them.

  • Speed up the website. When we tested 831 independent flooring store websites on a phone, the typical home page took 6.6 seconds to show its main content, and one in three took more than ten. Some shoppers leave before they ever see your showroom.
  • Make the next step obvious. A phone number you can tap, your address and hours, and one short way to ask a question, on every page.
  • Answer the phone. Return every missed call the same day. Check the voicemail greeting and the after-hours message.
  • Answer web requests within the hour when you are open, and first thing the next morning when you are not.
  • Follow up on estimates. Two days later and again a week later.

Step 2: be found by people shopping now

  • Google Business Profile. Complete, current and with the products you sell listed. See the Business Profile checklist.
  • Local SEO. A page for each main product and the towns you serve. See SEO for flooring companies.
  • Reviews. Ask every customer, with no reward attached.

Step 3: add paid leads you control

Search ads reach people who typed in what you sell today, in your town. You own the account, the ads and the phone number, and the lead comes only to you.

  • Run a campaign for each main product.
  • Block searches that never buy, such as jobs and do-it-yourself questions.
  • Send each ad to a page about that product.
  • Give ads their own phone number so you can count their calls.

Should you buy flooring leads?

Lead sellers collect requests from homeowners and sell them to contractors and stores. It can work as a small test. Go in with your eyes open, and ask these before you pay:

  1. Is each lead exclusive to me, or sold to several companies?
  2. How was the lead generated, and did the homeowner ask for a flooring quote in my area?
  3. Do I get credit for bad leads, such as wrong numbers and people who never asked?
  4. Is there a contract or a minimum, and can I stop at any time?
  5. Who owns the customer if I stop buying?

A shared lead goes to whoever calls first. If your store cannot call within minutes, shared leads will cost you more per sold job than they appear to.

How to price a lead

Work backward from a sold job.

  1. Decide what you can afford to spend to win one sold job.
  2. Find the share of real leads you turn into sold jobs.
  3. Multiply the two. That is the most a lead is worth to you.

Here is the math with made-up numbers. If a store can afford $300 to win a job and closes one real lead in four, a lead is worth up to $75 to that store. A source that charges $40 a lead and closes at that rate is worth keeping. A source that charges $40 but closes one in ten costs $400 per sold job and is not. Your numbers will be different. The method is the point, and it is laid out in full in our flooring marketing guide.

Track leads to sold jobs

  • Give each main source its own phone number.
  • Log every lead with where it came from.
  • Mark which ones became sold jobs.
  • Once a month, divide what each source cost by the jobs it sold.

FAQ

How do I get flooring leads?

Start with the leads you already lose: a slow website, a home page with no clear next step, missed calls and web requests nobody answers. Then add Google search ads aimed at buying searches in your town, and steady local SEO and review work. Count a lead only when a real person asks about a real project.

Is it worth buying flooring leads?

Sometimes, as a small test. Bought leads are often sold to several companies at once, so speed decides who wins. Before you pay, ask whether each lead is exclusive, how it was generated, whether bad leads are credited, and whether you can stop at any time. Then judge it by cost per sold job.

How much should a flooring lead cost?

There is no single fair price, because it depends on how many leads become sold jobs and what a job is worth to you. Work backward: decide what you can afford to spend to win one sold job, then multiply by the share of leads you close. That gives the most you should pay for a lead.

What counts as a flooring lead?

A real person asking about a real project, such as carpet for three bedrooms or a quote on vinyl plank for a kitchen. A wrong number, a vendor, a job seeker or an existing customer checking on an order is not a lead.

How fast should a flooring store respond to a lead?

The same hour during business hours, and first thing the next morning otherwise. People asking for a flooring quote usually ask more than one store, and the store that answers first with a clear next step has the advantage.
