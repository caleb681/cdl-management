Example 01

A sample three-priority growth audit

Illustrative situation: a flooring showroom sells carpet and luxury vinyl, offers installation and wants more relevant local inquiries. Its team is deciding whether to improve the existing website, add local SEO work or discuss advertising.

Make the next step visible on a phone

Example observation: the carpet page describes products, but its only contact link is in the footer. A shopper must leave the page or scroll to find how to ask about a measure.

Recommended change: put a clearly named estimate-request link near the product explanation, with a tap-to-call option and a short description of what happens after an inquiry.

Check before calling it complete: review the page on a small screen and with keyboard navigation. With the showroom's permission, submit a controlled test and confirm receipt with the intended team. A visible success message alone does not prove delivery.

Service decision: this may be a focused improvement to the current site. It does not, by itself, justify replacing the entire website. Explore website work.

Explain the flooring decision, not just the category

Example observation: a luxury vinyl page lists product types but leaves out the store's actual installation process, where it serves customers and how an installed estimate is prepared.

Recommended change: answer the questions the team hears: what to bring to a sample visit, how measurements work, and which site conditions may affect the estimate. Add genuine project photos when permission and accurate details are available.

Check before calling it complete: have the showroom confirm every product, installation and service-area statement. Do not imply a product is in stock, a job is scheduled or a warranty applies without the facts.

Service decision: useful category and service content can support both local SEO and AI search visibility. A list of nearby city names does not replace relevant local information.

Help a local shopper find the same store everywhere

Example observation: the website shows different Saturday hours from the showroom's public business listing.

Recommended change: confirm the correct regular and holiday hours with the team, then update the places within the agreed scope. Check the address, phone and directions link at the same time.

Check before calling it complete: read back the published information. Record any listing that still needs account access or review instead of marking everything fixed.

Service decision: accurate local details are a practical first step. More traffic has limited value if a shopper cannot tell when or where to visit. Read the Business Profile guide.

Each priority should lead to a decision

Record the page or listing, the observed issue, the proposed change, who must confirm it and how the change will be checked. If a public review cannot establish something, label it as a question for the account review.

Example 02

A showroom page that helps someone choose

A product page should help someone move from “I need new flooring” to a useful conversation with your team. This text-only concept shows the content order for a luxury vinyl page. It is an example, not a live client website or a tested conversion result.

Illustrative page concept

Find luxury vinyl that fits your rooms and routine.

Start with the spaces you are updating, how you use them and the look you like. A showroom conversation can narrow the options and identify the details needed for an installed estimate.

  • Bring your questions: busy rooms, pets, stairs, existing flooring and budget.
  • Compare what matters: appearance, care instructions and the manufacturer's stated product limitations.
  • Understand the estimate: confirm measurements, removal, subfloor preparation, transitions and installation scope for your project.

Suggested next step: “Ask about your flooring project,” paired with the showroom's confirmed contact route and what happens next.

What the finished page needs from the showroom

  • Its real location, service area, hours and available consultation or measurement process.
  • Accurate product and installation information reviewed by the people delivering it.
  • Genuine photography and permission to use it, with captions that explain what is shown.
  • A short, relevant inquiry path. Product catalogs, room visualizers, sample ordering and licensed content require their own access and scope checks.

Useful page content can work alongside a catalog. It should explain the local showroom's role without suggesting a licensed catalog or tool can automatically transfer from another provider.

Example 03

A monthly report that ends with a next step

Sample format: the fields below are deliberately unpopulated. They show the records and definitions needed to assess marketing, without presenting simulated numbers as results.

Start with the reporting frame

Show the showroom, channels included, reporting dates, comparison dates and data sources. Separate media spend from agency fees. Identify missing tracking, access changes or partial periods before comparing performance.

Example decision note

Illustrative situation: an ad for carpet installation leads to a general flooring homepage. The report recommends reviewing a matching page with the showroom's actual installation process and a clear inquiry route. Before expanding the campaign, confirm its conversion definitions and review inquiry quality for a complete, comparable period.

This is a decision example, not a finding about an existing CDL campaign or a claim of wasted spend.

Keep clicks, received inquiries, qualified inquiries, measures and sold jobs distinct. When several channels touch one customer, document the attribution rule rather than adding every platform's claimed sale together. If the connection to sales is unavailable, say what the report can establish and what remains unknown.

CRM integration is planned for later. These reporting examples do not imply that CDL currently connects to your CRM or has access to your customer records. Agree the permitted sources and reporting scope first.

The guidance behind the approach

Google recommends accurate local business information and applies its search foundations to generative AI features. Its advertising guidance distinguishes conversion actions used for optimization from observation. Those principles support careful definitions and useful content; they do not establish a particular showroom's results.
